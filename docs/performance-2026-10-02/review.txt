ElseMesh rendering review — 2 October 2026

RENDERER REVIEW SCOPE
Main frame/update sequencing; mesh collection/culling, transform propagation,
material sorting and shadow cascades; opaque/refraction/water passes and SSR;
ocean simulation and LOD; port batching and traffic; vegetation/tree animation
and wildlife; sky/cloud quality; postprocessing, AO, bloom, volumetric haze,
exposure and temporal upscaling; existing profiling and driving-review tools.

Three conservative implementation experiments:
1. Share stable main-scene transforms across passes within a frame, while retaining
   camera-dependent culling, callbacks, and independent auxiliary-scene updates.
2. Avoid the full-resolution half-float water depth conversion when SSR is off.
   Keep ordinary refraction colour/depth copies and immediately resume the
   conversion when reflections are enabled.
3. Skip unused AO, bloom reconstruction and haze work when those contributions
   are inactive. Preserve lens/exposure inputs and reset haze history on resume.

Larger follow-on opportunities identified: reducing the expensive water optical
work at distance; overdraw-aware opaque submission; distant tree animation work;
and effect-specific cloud/temporal-resolution budgets. These need separate
quality comparisons. Existing material batching and the already-rejected 64m
port batching experiment are not counted as new improvements.

CONTROLLED FOREGROUND FPS RESULTS
Intel Arc / Xe-LPG, Windows, Chrome 154. Balanced quality, 1600x900 output,
1200x675 internal rendering, TAA, shadows on, SSR off, AO 1, bloom 0.05.
Two baseline/candidate/candidate/baseline cycles per view; 60 warm-up and
180 measured foreground frames per run. Simulation clock fixed (dt=0);
some frame-driven animation continues. Automatic resolution was disabled.
All 48 runs complete: 4,320 measured frames per mode, with matching cameras,
time of day, sizes and final draw counts. No outliers were discarded.

View                     Baseline FPS   Optimized FPS   Change
Beach                         20.11          25.51       +26.85%
Village                       30.91          30.74        -0.53%
Underwater                    22.77          29.75       +30.64%
Forest villa                  20.33          24.56       +20.84%
Warehouse junction            23.78          30.15       +26.78%
Wide cargo harbour            22.64          24.86        +9.80%
Equal-frame pooled sample     22.95          27.34       +19.12%

FPS above is frames divided by elapsed sampled frame time, not the inverse
of the median frame time. Refresh quantization makes those differ materially.
Both cycles improved in each of the five winning views. Village's cycles had
opposite signs; no FPS change is demonstrated there. A separate earlier beach
comparison also improved average FPS: 20.60 to 24.87 (+20.7%).

This is a measured fixed-camera mean-FPS improvement, not a game-wide,
moving-camera, cold-start, thermal-soak, mobile or multiplayer qualification.
Severe hitches remain unexplained. In the six-view run, frames over 150ms rose
from 6 to 17. Cargo p99 worsened from 73.8 to 107.5ms and its worst frame from
145.2 to 670.1ms. Do not infer a worst-stutter improvement from the higher FPS.

An initial run with approximately one-second browser callbacks was aborted
and excluded. Bringing the benchmark page forward resolved the throttling.
All complete foreground runs were free of that pattern. Separate GPU timing
attempts with incomplete readbacks were rejected; they do not supply FPS data.

Settled before/after screenshots of beach, wide harbour and underwater views
showed no obvious candidate-specific visual regression; no WebGPU errors were
observed. This is visual review, not pixel equality. Lens droplets retained
from a prior underwater view were present in both harbour images.

Evidence: elsemesh-fps-balanced-six-views.json, elsemesh-fps-summary.json,
elsemesh-fps-balanced-beach.json, and elsemesh-measured-source.json.

ADDITIONAL VALIDATION AND REPRODUCTION
All three changes are enabled by default. The original measurement source
manifest records the pre-promotion diagnostic defaults; the shipped manifest
records the final files. Renderer logic is unchanged by promotion.

The GPU recorder was repaired to reserve readback slots and bound queued work.
The corrected component experiment completed 32 runs / 2,880 timestamp frames
with zero dropped, invalid or failed readbacks. It used a separate AO=0,
bloom=0 scenario on both baseline and candidate. The water-depth variant removed
the 'opaque depth half' pass; the inactive-effects variant removed the GTAO,
AO-depth, AO-blur and bloom-up labels while refraction remained. Individual total
GPU times varied substantially; this is pass-removal evidence, not proof that
each individual change improves mean FPS. The combined foreground measurement
above is the publication criterion. Do not turn GPU elapsed milliseconds into
a displayed-FPS claim.

Additional settled visual comparisons covered AO=0/bloom=0 on the beach and
High quality with SSR enabled at the wide harbour. No obvious rendering loss
or WebGPU errors were observed. High was selected using the in-game quality
control; its settings record therefore has requestedQuality=balanced (original
URL), actual profile=high, renderScale=1, SSR=1 and internal size 1600x900.

Run npm run dev and open the printed URL with
?bench&rendererReview&quality=balanced&noAudio . Keep the browser window visible
and foreground. Release game mouse capture before using the review controls.
Select All six views / All optimizations / Existing quality settings, then
Run foreground ABBA. The recorder retains every sample in its downloadable
JSON; incomplete/hidden/resized runs are invalid. The GPU button runs a separate
instrumented comparison with capture backpressure; it is not foreground FPS.
